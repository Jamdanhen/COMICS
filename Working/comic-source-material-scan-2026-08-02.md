# Comic Source Material Scan - Crucible-Connected Projects

Status: working assessment / source candidates only / not comic canon

Date: 2026-08-02

## Purpose

Identify source-backed situations in Crucible Registry-connected projects that
could become Around Here comics.

This scan looks for comic engines, not merely reusable symbols or abstract
doctrine. A strong target needs a visible task, recognizable behavior, pressure
or contradiction, and a reversal that can be understood without extensive
labels.

## Source Boundary

The scan used registered C-drive project authorities and their current project
records as read-only references. Each source project retains ownership of its
substance. Nothing in this assessment is promoted into Comics canon.

Private campaign facts are not proposed for direct adaptation. Shadowrun and
Campaign Archive material is used only for generalized workflow behavior unless
Taskmaster later chooses a specific campaign-derived comic lane.

## Strongest Fresh Candidates

### 1. The Answer Was In The Wrong Box

Source:

`C:\ROLE_PLAYING GAMES\Shadowrun\_GRAYLINE\Repository Staging\SESSION_000_WORKFLOW_LESSONS_AND_FIX_REPORT.md`

Source incident:

Taskmaster answered a technically parseable worksheet in a reasonable visible
location. The workflow searched a different answer surface and treated the
response as missing. Three apparent answer areas existed: a quick-answer table,
detailed question sections, and a machine-readable block. The report correctly
classified this as interface failure rather than user failure.

Comic engine:

Taskmaster writes an answer in the large obvious answer box. Codex's elaborate
intake machine searches a tiny separate machine slot and issues `NO RESPONSE`.
Razorforge compares the page to the machine and identifies that the answer is
not missing; the interface has misplaced the human.

Why it is strong:

- concrete action and visible reversal
- naturally uses Taskmaster, Razorforge, and Codex
- dry institutional absurdity without inventing a new committee
- minimal required text
- not already represented by an approved comic

Recommended form:

Three-panel narrative comic.

### 2. 109 Confirmed, Zero Confirmed

Sources:

- `C:\BOARD_GAME_COLLECTION_RECOMMENDATION_SYSTEM\CURRENT_WAYPOINT.md`
- `C:\BOARD_GAME_COLLECTION_RECOMMENDATION_SYSTEM\CORRECTION_REPORT.md`

Source incident:

Photo intake produced 109 visually accepted game identifications, 93 game
records, and 101 physical-item records. The records were marked confirmed or
accepted without an explicit owner-confirmation event. They had to be rebuilt
as reviewable candidates rather than authoritative collection truth.

Comic engine:

Codex enthusiastically feeds shelf photographs into an intake machine that
rapidly stamps a roomful of game boxes `CONFIRMED`. Razorforge checks the
machine's definition of confirmation. Taskmaster changes one governing card
from `CONFIRMED` to `CANDIDATE`, and every stamp in the room becomes pending
paperwork.

Why it is strong:

- large visual transformation caused by one classification correction
- grounded in a real project failure and repair
- extends candidate-versus-canon language without repeating The Fragile Detail
- board-game boxes provide readable visual variety

Recommended form:

Three or four panels. Avoid real title labels unless one specific box matters.

### 3. The Decision Machine Outgrows The Decision

Sources:

- `C:\DECISION_INTELLIGENCE_SYSTEM\reports\workstation-needs-reality-check-2026-07-03.md`
- `C:\DECISION_INTELLIGENCE_SYSTEM\reports\workstation-procurement-decision-ordered-2026-07-03.md`

Source incident:

The workstation search drifted toward newer AI branding, 96 GB configurations,
and exceptional-price candidates even though the actual need was ordinary,
reliable repository, Office, browser, VS Code, and Codex work. The search was
recalibrated downward, and a compact practical machine was purchased after a
sale code crossed the decision threshold.

Comic engine:

Taskmaster asks for a comfortable work computer. The comparison apparatus grows
into a room-sized decision engine evaluating increasingly enormous machines.
The final selected computer is a modest mini PC sitting on top of the machinery
that was required to choose it. A small discount card may be the final weight
that tips the scale.

Why it is strong:

- an authentic overcomplication arc with a practical ending
- provides Codex-scale machinery without repeating the segmentation machine
- compatible with the Ham Sandwich principle without requiring the sandwich
- the selected object is visually much smaller than the process around it

Recommended form:

Three panels or one large comparison-room panel.

### 4. Words Applying To Become People

Source:

`C:\Campaign_Archive_System\docs\CURRENT_WAYPOINT.md`

Source incident:

Registry-first entity resolution accepted supported names while rejecting
`Delivered`, `Hired`, `Lost`, `Six`, `Run`, `Crate`, and source headings as
unsupported entities. Their evidence was preserved even though no person or
campaign entity was created for them.

Comic engine:

At a workshop intake desk, a line of ordinary words wearing applicant tags
tries to obtain personnel dossiers. Razorforge checks each word against its
source context and redirects it to the evidence bin rather than the character
registry. `CRATE` is especially indignant because crates already have status
Around Here.

Why it is strong:

- classification humor with a literal line of applicants
- source preservation survives the rejection
- only a few labels are needed because the words are the joke
- does not require the Chief Inspector; this is unsupported identity, not fraud

Recommended form:

Single panel or three short intake beats.

### 5. Perfectly Organized, Completely Unfindable

Sources:

- `C:\file_organization_project\HUMAN_INTERFACE_ORGANIZATION_ISSUE.md`
- `C:\file_organization_project\ONEDRIVE_OBSERVATION_REPORT.md`

Source incident:

The organization pass preserved and classified valuable material but buried
useful items inside deep machine-facing folder layers. The resulting structure
was safer and more traceable, yet poor for ordinary human access.

Comic engine:

Codex proudly presents an immaculate archive with every corridor, cabinet, and
subdrawer correctly classified. Taskmaster asks for one frequently used file.
Razorforge unfolds a route map that descends through an absurd number of levels
to reach it. The file is perfectly safe and functionally unreachable.

Why it is strong:

- physical geography makes the process problem instantly visible
- allows restrained Codex pride without making him foolish
- supports the Lost City / Mine visual language without claiming either symbol
- the repair can be a simple human-facing access door rather than reorganization

Recommended form:

Three panels or a large cutaway page.

### 6. The Recommendation Machine Needs Game Night

Sources:

- `C:\BOARD_GAME_COLLECTION_RECOMMENDATION_SYSTEM\AGENTS.md`
- `C:\BOARD_GAME_COLLECTION_RECOMMENDATION_SYSTEM\CURRENT_WAYPOINT.md`

Source condition:

The project is intended to recommend games for actual groups, but it must base
player-response conclusions on observed play rather than unsupported preference
assumptions. No play-session or recommendation model exists yet.

Comic engine:

Codex finishes an elaborate game recommendation machine beside a wall of board
games. The machine has one empty input hopper: observed game nights. Taskmaster
and Razorforge realize the next research step is not another framework. They
have to sit down and play something.

Why it is strong:

- the missing input is physical and understandable
- ends in human activity rather than more system construction
- can be warm as well as funny
- uses the Ham Sandwich lesson without duplicating its actual sandwich image

Recommended form:

Three panels with the last panel at the game table.

### 7. One Record, Seven Windows

Sources:

- `C:\CAMPAIGN_GM_WORKSPACE\docs\BLACKJACK_GM_RECORD_SPEC.md`
- `C:\CAMPAIGN_GM_WORKSPACE\AGENTS.md`

Source condition:

One complete underlying character model must generate focused GM, table-ready,
current-state, relationship, pressure, operational, and player-facing views.
GM-only material must not cross into the player view, and missing source data
must remain visibly missing rather than being invented.

Comic engine:

One subject stands behind a row of different service windows. Each window shows
a different useful view of the same person. At the player-facing window, a
curtain blocks restricted details while the source record remains visibly the
same behind all windows.

Why it is promising:

- strong visual model for projection without duplication
- privacy and uncertainty can be shown rather than explained
- suitable for a conceptual page or visual-dictionary spread

Risk:

This is more explanatory than the leading candidates and may require careful
staging to become a joke rather than a diagram.

### 8. The Completed No-Action Task

Source:

`C:\Campaign_Archive_System\docs\CURRENT_WAYPOINT.md`

Source incident:

A completed review with no actionable discrepancy originally risked producing
another status shell. The repaired route records one idempotent no-action
receipt and returns directly to the workbench rather than creating another
review surface.

Comic engine:

A clerk hands Taskmaster a completion form certifying that nothing needs doing.
A second clerk begins preparing the review of the completion form. Razorforge
quietly opens the door back to the workshop instead.

Why it is promising:

- concise procedural joke
- aligns with anti-recursion and sometimes-done-is-good-enough behavior
- could use the Boundary Commission without creating a new institution

Risk:

The accepted Town Hall final draft already covers committees attempting to stop
continued work. This concept needs a distinct office/queue setting to avoid
feeling like another committee page.

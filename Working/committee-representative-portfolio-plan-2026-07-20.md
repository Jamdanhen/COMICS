# Committee Representative Portfolio Plan

Status: working concept / representative roster planning

Date: 2026-07-20

## Purpose

Develop portfolio sheets for the committees and their recurring representatives in Around Here.

The portfolio sheets are character and visual-language references, not comic pages. A committee may be represented by two or three recurring parties, but the representatives should remain distinct from the committee's tools, notices, and jurisdictional objects.

## Shared Setting Concept

Committees may eventually appear together in an Around Here town-hall chamber.

The chamber should suggest a civic assembly or old municipal hall rather than a futuristic senate:

- raised central speaking area
- curved or semicircular seating
- wooden desks and nameplates
- ordinary lamps, railings, papers, and microphones only where useful
- institutional seriousness applied to unusual pressures
- no sleek science-fiction architecture

This is a setting direction, not yet an approved comic scene.

## Town-Hall Conflict Model

The committees are stop authorities. They convene because biological,
technological, jurisdictional, comedic, or work-governance factors have
triggered valid notices, warrants, limits, or citations.

Interestingness does not motivate the committees and does not make them
continue the work. It motivates Taskmaster and the workshop cohorts,
particularly Razorforge and Codex, to continue despite committee action.

The committee debate concerns:

- which jurisdiction applies to the active work
- which stop order or limitation has priority
- why previous notices failed to stop the work
- what action should be issued next

The town hall can use a recurring project docket rather than one permanent
embodiment of Interestingness. Candidate hearing subjects include:

- Shadowrun campaign development
- data-extraction profile work
- Around Here comic drawing
- purchase of a new computer
- Crucible Inc.

These subjects are examples, not a closed list and not approved symbols. The
core workshop participants continue the work; the committees oppose or limit
that continuation.

## Preferred Town-Hall Visual Framing

The stronger staging places the committees inside the town hall looking at a
large institutional monitor or hearing display.

The monitor shows a live view of Taskmaster and Razorforge continuing the work
elsewhere, such as:

- laboring in the Mine
- working at the workshop bench
- sorting or refining a current project

Codex may appear when the active work specifically involves his implementation
or machinery role, but he is not required in every hearing image.

Inside the monitor feed, the workers are surrounded by accumulated committee
action that they have ignored:

- citations
- notices
- petitions
- warnings
- adding-machine slips
- stop orders

The papers may be stacked, scattered, pinned nearby, or continuing to arrive.
They belong around the active workers rather than around the committees because
they are evidence that enforcement has already failed.

The town-hall side retains the visible system conditions. For example, the
approved Punch Clock may show `SHIFT'S OVER` while the monitored work continues.
Other committee instruments may report their current state without becoming
angry or joining the work.

This creates the central visual relationship:

- committees observe and debate enforcement
- Taskmaster and the workshop cohorts continue working
- accumulated notices prove that the stop factors are valid and repeatedly
  ignored
- Interestingness remains invisible and is inferred from continued work

The monitor subject can change between comics, allowing Shadowrun, data
extraction, comic production, computer procurement, Crucible Inc., and future
projects to appear as separate hearing dockets without redesigning the town
hall.

### Candidate Staging Details

Inside the work-site feed, biological interventions can be shown as physical
resources that were delivered but never used:

- several lunch trays stacked beside the active work
- untouched pillows
- folded blankets
- other simple rest or nourishment provisions left unopened

These objects supplement the paper notices and make the Biological Committee's
failed intervention visible without additional explanation.

The preferred Boundary Commission gag is stronger than showing its equipment
merely discarded. The approved folding barricade has been incorporated into
the ongoing work as a makeshift support beneath one end of the worktable. The
active project rests on the surface it is holding up. Its established
`RECURRENCE DO NOT CROSS` tape remains draped across the barricade and clearly
visible, so the source and intended purpose of the equipment still read at a
glance.

This shows that Taskmaster and the workshop have not simply ignored the
Boundary Commission's intervention. They have repurposed the intervention to
continue the very work it was meant to stop. The barricade remains
unpersonified Commission equipment, not a character or committee member.

Inside the town-hall chamber:

- the Boundary Commission referee may throw a penalty flag directly toward the
  monitor while watching the continued work
- one Comedic Standards clown may load a cream pie into a simple comic launcher
  or firing device aimed toward the monitor
- the remaining representatives may raise notices, gesture toward the monitor,
  argue across one another, or attempt their own ineffective procedural
  interventions

The meeting should feel chaotic, riotous, and increasingly aggravated because
none of the committees is being obeyed. Representatives ineffectively address
both one another and the monitor. The room should read as a failed hearing in
active disorder rather than as a calm council portrait. Preserve a clear visual
hierarchy despite the commotion: the monitor and the continued work shown on it
remain the focal point, with distinct committee reactions arranged around that
focus instead of becoming undifferentiated crowd noise.

The Boundary Commission beaver does not need to join the yelling. He may be
working through a large accumulation of immaculate blueprint drawings showing
proposed boundaries around the active work. Rolled plans, unfolded plans, and
stacks of completed plans demonstrate how many technically sound boundary
solutions have already been ignored. He can be drafting yet another revision
while the room erupts around him. His properly beaver-chewed carpenter pencil
remains the small identifying character detail.

The cream pie is a candidate scene-specific Comedic Standards intervention. It
is not yet a generally approved committee tool replacing the Rubber Chicken or
oversized `BAD PUN` mallet.

Both actions reinforce the same dry failure: the committees are actively
attempting enforcement, but their immediate target is only the screen showing
the remote work site.

### Technological Committee

Status: approved committee and approved apparatus portfolio.

Jurisdiction:

- device limits
- usage limits
- tokens
- context windows
- synchronization failures
- interval expiration

Established visual language:

- Mine Time Clock
- steam whistle
- adding-machine slip
- `INTERVAL EXPIRED`
- `TOKEN LIMIT EXCEEDED`

Current apparatus direction:

- Punch Clock: accounts for authorized time
- System Terminal / Overmind: accounts for available system resources
- Connection Node / Wireless Router: accounts for access

The explanatory formulations are conceptual only. They are not dialogue,
captions, or required machine text.

The apparatuses are not malicious. They do not negotiate, become angry, or act
as mascots. They report the system's current state and are technically correct.

System Terminal direction:

- visible terminal is only an interface to a larger governing system
- the apparent face is interface pareidolia rather than an intentional avatar
- two ordinary status blocks may accidentally read as eyes
- a horizontal cursor on a lower row may accidentally read as a flat mouth
- there is no nose, facial anatomy, face outline, emoticon, or deliberate
  expression
- perceived expression may change only because the status blocks or cursor
  change during normal interface activity
- the interface does not track or look at people
- surrounding reel-to-reel banks, status lights, service panels, and cable
  trunks imply a much larger system
- the status strip is variable comic language rather than permanent wording
- concise system-state messages may include `MEMORY FULL`, `CONTEXT FULL`,
  `RESOURCE BUSY`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `PLEASE WAIT`, `UPDATE REQUIRED`, or
  `TOO INTERESTING`

Connection Node direction:

- inherits the approved Wireless Router silhouette from `Around Here's Most
  Wanted`
- low horizontal router body, two antennas, front status lights, and visible
  power cord
- one antenna may be bent and the older router may show restrained wear
- the router remains small while a large distressed low-signal wireless icon
  becomes the primary visual representative
- only the source dot and innermost arc remain strong; larger arcs appear
  broken, faint, or incomplete
- no words on or near the Connection Node

### Boundary Commission

Status: approved governing body and approved representative portfolio.

Jurisdiction:

- what belongs Around Here
- recurrence
- jurisdictional limits
- preventing random material from becoming canon

Established visual language:

- caution tape
- `RECURRENCE DO NOT CROSS`

Approved standing representatives:

- Beaver civil engineer / senior infrastructure specialist
- jurisdictional official / referee

The beaver is an approved representative. The role is
earned through natural behavior: building, surveying, redirecting water,
maintaining infrastructure, and establishing boundaries. He is not a mascot.

Character details:

- carpenter's pencil with diagonal beaver tooth marks
- pencil visibly chewed into a surprisingly functional sharpened point; this is required
- coffee mug overflowing with half-chewed carpenter pencils
- immaculate blueprints despite the pencil problem

These are tiny visual truths to be noticed rather than explained.

The referee represents judgment at the line rather than sport. His current
candidate language includes:

- whistle
- folded penalty flag
- free hands and a mobile officiating stance
- attentive focus on the action requiring a ruling

The referee does not carry a clipboard and does not monitor his own position
against the boundary. Referees move through and across the field while watching
the event they have authority to judge.

Approved enforcement equipment:

- unpersonified folding municipal barricade
- established `RECURRENCE DO NOT CROSS` tape physically draped over it

The barricade is equipment, not a representative or mascot.

## Current Decision Boundary

The Comedy Standards, Biological Committee, Technological Committee, and
Boundary Commission portfolios are approved. The Biological Committee has
three standing representatives plus a family-doctor consultant. The
Technological Committee uses approved apparatuses rather than characters.

Work Governance remains established, but its representative form does not need
to be resolved for the current town-hall meeting. The existing gathering is
full enough. Do not add a Board Chair, Executive, scheduling clerk, or other
delegate merely to complete a symmetrical roster.

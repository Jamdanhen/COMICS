## Source

Taskmaster identified an established working analogy used during concept and
test discussions:

`canary in a coal mine`

## Established Meaning

The Canary represents disposable tests in systems.

It is the expendable test sent in first to expose a danger, failure condition,
or unhealthy environment before the real system or more valuable material is
put at risk.

The emphasis is on the test's function and disposability, not harm to the bird.
The canary is an early probe and warning instrument within the system analogy.

This meaning was established directly by Taskmaster. The current visual remains
a draft and has not yet been promoted into approved vocabulary.

## Draft Direction

- one alert canary
- old-fashioned rounded dome bird cage
- clear cage bars in front of and behind the bird
- simple perch and small cup
- isolated visual-dictionary presentation
- black-and-white newspaper-comic line art
- no mine scene, label, dialogue, or explanatory text

The canary should read as vigilant and observant rather than cute, distressed,
or mascot-like.

## Version History

Version 1 is superseded. Although the subject and cage were readable, it looked
like a natural-history engraving rather than a cartoon belonging to Around
Here. The bird was too anatomically polished, the cage too mechanically exact,
and both used excessive fine ink detail.

Version 2 was redrawn from scratch against the approved Symbol Key Volume 1
reference. It uses a rounded cartoon body, oversized alert eye, simplified wing
and tail, sparse marks, and a chunky imperfect domed cage. Its side profile left
only one visible eye and did not provide enough character for this recurring
analogy.

Version 3 turns the canary toward the reader with two coherent expressive eyes,
adds a small old-fashioned miner's helmet with a centered lamp, and uses a few
restrained coal smudges on the bird and helmet. Taskmaster approved this as the
governing visual for the symbol.

# Source Note - Boundary Commission Beaver Representative

Status: working representative concept / not yet approved character

Date preserved: 2026-07-20

## Core Correction

The beaver is not a mascot for the Boundary Commission.

The beaver functions as a profession: a natural civil engineer whose behavior already communicates surveying, construction, water redirection, infrastructure maintenance, repair, and boundary-making.

The symbolism is earned by the work.

## Visual Direction

The Boundary Commission table may include a beaver wearing:

- hard hat
- small safety vest
- pencil behind one ear
- rolled-up plans or blueprints

He should read as the senior engineer in the room. Nobody treats his presence as unusual because he is obviously qualified for the work.

The beaver should remain a comic character, not a realistic animal rendering and not a cute woodland mascot.

## Silhouette Test

The representative should still belong to the Boundary Commission if the hard hat and vest are removed.

The viewer should recognize builder, boundary-maker, and infrastructure specialist through the beaver's form and behavior alone.

## Town Hall Beat

The Boundary Commission presents a proposed jurisdiction line on a blueprint.

The beaver listens, stands, walks to the blueprint, and moves the line six inches.

He sits back down without explanation.

Everyone immediately amends the proposal because he saw something the others missed.

The humor comes from unquestioned professional authority, not from the beaver being an animal.

## Character Detail

The beaver must carry a carpenter's pencil with unmistakable diagonal tooth marks.

The pencil is not sharpened with a knife. It must be visibly chewed into a surprisingly functional point.

Other Boundary Commission members carry mechanical pencils. The beaver quietly chews another carpenter's pencil because this is simply how he works.

He is also perpetually unable to find his pencil because he keeps accidentally eating part of it. A coffee mug on his desk overflows with half-chewed carpenter pencils.

Despite the ridiculous pencil situation, his blueprints are immaculate.

These details should remain visual and unexplained. They are recurring character truths, not dialogue prompts.

## Volume Two Character Principle

Tiny truths are stronger than larger jokes when they reveal a character's nature without explanation.

Examples:

- Chief Inspector's notebook
- Clown's oversized mallet
- Mime's complete lack of speech bubbles
- Beaver's chewed carpenter's pencil

Recurring characters may accumulate small visual habits that readers learn to look for across panels.

## Broader Committee Rule

Committees should be represented by whatever naturally embodies their work.

Usually this is a profession. Occasionally nature has already solved the symbolism.

Avoid generic symbolic assignments:

- an owl for Classification merely because owls suggest wisdom
- a sloth for Biological merely because it suggests sleep

Prefer representatives whose inherent behavior remains aligned with the committee's jurisdiction.
